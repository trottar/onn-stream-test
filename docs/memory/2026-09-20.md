# 2026-09-20

Phase C suspended. The premise it was built on is falsified.

## C3.L3a Part 2 ran, and the probe has three defects

The gameplay acceptance session ran to completion: 8 traversals, 16
transitions, zero FEC/audio/controller deltas across the whole session. That
lifecycle result is real and is a `C3.L4` readiness answer — repeated
transitions do not degrade a subsystem that a single one does not.

Two of the report's headline numbers are probe defects, not findings:

- **the mark-association window anchors on sequence start.** A ramp runs ~11 s,
  so its 2.5 s window closes long before it finishes. Four marks landed
  1.5-1.9 s after a sequence *ended* — textbook reaction lags — and all were
  scored "attributed to nothing". The bias runs toward making transitions look
  invisible, which is the worst possible direction;
- **telemetry field paths were wrong.** They were built from
  `c2_stream_telemetry_runtime.json`, which is the C2 probe's own output
  artifact, not the endpoint response. The live shape is `receiver.recent_fps`,
  not `measurements.receiver_recent_fps`. Every sample returned None, the
  settle test required fps near 60, so settling was never measured. "8/8 never
  settled" measures nothing.
- **the picture rating used an unanchored 1-5 scale.** The user rated out of
  10; the prompt showed a bare `[1-5]` with no anchors and no statement of what
  1 or 5 meant. Their answers were 5/10, 3/10 and 2/10 for 6000/5500/5000, not
  5/5, 3/5 and 2/5. Read correctly the ratings and the accept/reject booleans
  agree; the "contradiction" was an artifact of assuming the wrong scale.

All raw marks and per-cycle timings are retained in the state file, so the
session is re-scorable without replaying.

Aggregating every decoder session on disk of >= 15 s — 47 sessions,
2026-09-16 to 2026-09-20, all at the 7000 kbps reference, most with no actuator
activity:

## The regression the user felt is real, and it is in the tail

Split by client code epoch: across **21 sessions before the 2026-09-19
flattening the worst stall was 338 ms**; after, it is **7,341 ms**, with 3,783,
1,997, 1,271 and 1,062 also on the board. The median moved 231 -> 392 ms. The
tail rose about twentyfold.

Session length does not explain it: a 16.7-minute pre-flatten session maxed at
331 ms, a 14.2-minute post session at 7,341 ms.

Decode spikes, fps and stale drops are **identical across every epoch**, so
neither the flattening nor `C3.L2b` caused the chronic decode fault. That one
predates all of Phase C.

Epoch B — flattened, pre-`C3.L2b` — has four sessions, two of them
sub-30-second fragments. **Too thin to separate the two changes.** `C3.L2b` is
the stronger suspect on mechanism: it added per-frame and per-packet
bookkeeping to `AvcLowLatencyDecoder` and `RtpH264Receiver`, i.e. the hot path,
while `ANDROID-FLAT` changed only directory layout and left Kotlin package
declarations, and therefore bytecode semantics, untouched. The flattening did
force a full rebuild, so it is not excluded.

The irony is on the record: the instrumentation added to measure stalls is the
prime suspect for causing them.

## Mechanism for the decode fault

The decoder holds **11-13 frames in flight** — 180-220 ms of pipeline depth
before any decode work is counted — while `latest_feed_delay_ms` is 0 and
`input_waits` stays in the tens. The client feeds promptly and is never
starved; the decoder is buffering for throughput, which is right for video
playback and wrong for interactive streaming. `stale_output_drops` are frames
decoded too late to display: 2,461 of 50,515 in the long session.

`MediaFormat.KEY_LOW_LATENCY` is the API for exactly this, and
`c2.realtek.video.avc.decoder` reports it unsupported.

## C3.L2c is reopened, on the wrong-statistic error

The single low-latency session is the best on record on every distributional
metric: **159 spikes/min against 1,828-2,723 for all 46 others**, best fps
(59.0), lowest drop rate (0.15%), `stale_output_drops` 16 against 191 in the
comparable non-low-latency session.

It was rolled back because `max_output_gap_ms` moved 359 -> 385 — one
worst-case sample per session. The falsification record correctly noted that
`max_codec_ms` is not a proxy for `max_output_gap_ms`; the error was the
unstated converse, that **`max_output_gap_ms` is not a proxy for how the stream
plays either.** One sample overruled thousands.

## Decision: baseline before adaptation

Phase C is **suspended, not failed**. None of the three faults is a bandwidth
fault, so adaptive bitrate control adjusts the one variable that is not the
problem. The user's requirement, recorded because it is the actual
requirement: seamless lag-free local play, with remote play unreachable
without it — a link that stalls 7.3 s locally has no headroom for WAN latency,
jitter and real loss.

The target is numeric and distributional, with no perceptual gate: spikes
>20 ms under 200/min, fps at or above 59.5, max gap at or under 100 ms, stale
drops under 20/min, loss under 10/min, audio underruns under 5/min.

Order of work: (1) settle the tail regression with a one-variable `C3.L2b`
revert; (2) fix the decoder path, reopening `C3.L2c` judged on the
distribution, and attack the 11-13 frame in-flight depth which the low-latency
flag alone did not fix; (3) record the physical link type, then transport;
(4) audio; (5) the pre-registered client decision.

**Pre-registered:** if after steps 1-3 a healthy decode path on a wired link
cannot reach under 200 spikes/min and 59.5 fps, the onn is the ceiling and the
client changes. Written down now so it is reached by measurement rather than
attrition.

## `D-BASE-P1` — stale-drop threshold characterized (night, unattended)

Record: `evidence/D_BASE_P1_STALE_THRESHOLD_2026-09-20.md`. Knob:
`patches/D-BASE-P1_STALE_THRESHOLD_PROBE_KNOB.md`. Reports under
`evidence/d_base_p1_2026-09-20/`. **CHARACTERIZED — nothing accepted, the
product default is 60 and unchanged.**

A measurement, not a product change. The hard-coded `60L` became a
constructor parameter defaulting to 60, settable for the probe through
`am start -n …/.MainActivity --ei privyhub.stale_output_ms N`, which the
launcher forwards into the stream intent. The companion was not touched.
The build also added `decoder.rx_to_output_histogram_ms`, a 10-bucket
histogram of receive-to-output latency over rendered frames — the number
the probe was really after.

Thirteen sessions, **none rejected** (no session had a sequence resync at
all; largest `max_output_gap_ms` 387 ms against the 1,000 ms line).

| threshold | rendered fps | received-AU fps | stale/min | rendered frames > 60 ms |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **60 (default)** | **59.65** | 59.83 | 9.3 | **0.000 %** |
| 90 | 59.56 | 59.67 | 3.1 | 0.143 % |
| 120 | 59.06 | 59.41 | 1.1 | 0.175 % |
| 200 | 59.27 | 59.39 | 0.0 | 0.279 % |

**The premise is retired.** Group A's "5.5 % of frames exceed 60 ms and are
dropped, 3.5 of the 4.5 fps deficit" was measured before `C3.L2c`. On the
current build the policy discards **6, 17 and 15 frames** in three ~97 s
control sessions, so recovering every one would add **0.06-0.18 fps** — a
bound from the control arm alone — and the arm medians show no gain at all;
fps drifts down with received-AU fps, not with the threshold.

**The cost of raising it is exactly where you would expect and nowhere
else.** p50 and p90 of rendered-frame latency are <= 20 ms in every arm; only
p99.9 moves, <= 40 ms at the default to <= 120 ms at 200. `max_output_gap_ms`
shows no trend (174 / 188 / 240 / 204), consistent with a transport-owned
tail. **60 already meets `rendered fps >= 59.5` with no rendered frame above
60 ms at all**, so on this evidence there is no reason to change it — and
the decision is the user's.

**One confound, named:** the interleave repeated a fixed 60 → 90 → 120 →
200 order, so each threshold always sat in the same cycle position. The
`spike_20_ms` arm differences (67.5 to 204.5 per minute) come from that and
not from the threshold, which cannot affect that counter — it is
incremented before the drop decision. Rotate the order in any re-run. The
conclusions do not rest on the cross-arm comparison.

**Limits:** thirteen idle attract sessions on a quiet link, n = 3 per arm,
no perceptual observation. On a session with real transport trouble the
policy may have far more to do.

**60**. Teardown per `TOOLS.md`: game ended from the client, banner

## Battery `C5` — receiver resync and IDR acceptance, read (night, unattended)

Record: `investigations/C5_RESYNC_IDR_ACCEPTANCE_READ_2026-09-20.md`.
Script and result under `evidence/c5_2026-09-20/`. **Analysis only — no
production code changed, nothing built, nothing installed, no session run.**

**The 195-332 ms is two costs, and the corpus separates them.** The base
cost is one GOP: the receiver re-arms mid-GOP and must wait for the
encoder's next scheduled IDR, with no way to ask for one (the encoder is an
external FFmpeg CLI with no control socket, `C3.L0`). On top of that,
`RtpH264Receiver.kt:2118` requires an access unit to be **complete** before
it can satisfy the IDR wait, so an IDR that lost a single packet is
discarded whole and the receiver waits another GOP.

Corpus since 2026-09-16 — 100 sessions carrying the per-discontinuity
lists, 167 discontinuities, 151 with a recorded first IDR:

| type | n | p50 | p90 | max | > 250 ms | > 500 ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `sequence_resync` | 104 | **204.5** | **484.2** | **2,555** | **32** | **10** |
| `ssrc_change` (actuator) | 47 | **27.0** | 45.2 | 76 | 0 | 0 |

**If the receiver simply took the next in-GOP IDR nothing could exceed
250 ms.** Thirty-one per cent do, out to ten GOPs. Each ~250 ms step is one
IDR seen and rejected.

**Two candidates falsified from evidence already on disk.** It is not
waiting for parameter sets: A1-live counted 41 SPS and 41 PPS against 40
IDRs in a 10 s on-wire sample, so they repeat with every IDR. And it is not
decoder output being stale-dropped: everything discarded during the wait is
dropped before `onAccessUnit`, and the decoder is neither flushed nor
reconfigured on resync — `onStreamDiscontinuity` only marks the
slow-event window.

Supporting: the accepted first IDR was FEC-recovered once in 104 resyncs
(XOR 8+1 cannot repair a 14-packet burst); and **bigger jumps recover
faster** — median 100 ms for jumps >= 256 packets against 210 ms for
smaller ones, because a large jump is an outage that has *ended* while a
small one is often loss still in progress.

**Proposed, not implemented, not authorized:** a counter of IDR access
units rejected while `waitingForIdr` (confirms or falsifies the
hypothesis in one session), then a bounded fallback that accepts an
incomplete IDR after one extra GOP — which trades an unbounded freeze for a
briefly corrupt picture and therefore needs a perceptual check, not just
numbers.

Record: `evidence/D_BASE_P2_AUDIO_UNDERRUN_BURST_2026-09-20.md`. Probe:

and `audio.first_video_output_elapsed_ms`. Product audio behaviour
unchanged.

**The burst is the start of the session, and nothing else.** Five 120 s
attract sessions, none rejected (zero discontinuities in all five): a
median **98.7 % of underruns in the first 3 s**, 80 % by 1,512 ms, ending
the tick the audio queue first fills. `queue_depth` is **0** for every tick

(median 5,757 ms) and ends at the first real PCM write (median 1,800 ms) —
which lands a median **1,013 ms after the first video frame**.

```

[ 963, 42, 0, 53, 0,  5]
```

**"113/min" was a fixed burst divided by a short session, and "~17/min
steady state" was the same artifact at a longer duration.** Across 177
sessions `underruns` is flat at 116-162 from the 0-40 s bucket to the
300 s+ bucket while `prolonged_starvation_events` (36 → 749) and
`concealed_underruns` (378 → 5,382) scale with duration. Spearman
`underruns_pm` against duration: **-0.725**.

**Three corrections to what was believed.** Audio loss does not drive
underruns (rho 0.113 against `lost_packets`; -0.414 against
`avg_queue_residence_ms` — a *shallow* queue, i.e. starvation). The
steady-state underruns (2-12 per session) do **not** coincide with video
slow events — 1 of 8 late ticks landed within 750 ms of one, against 64
slow events per session. And **`prolonged_starvation_events` is a second
phenomenon**, absent from the burst, steady at 1-2 per 10 s afterwards,
rho 0.195 — unexplained, and worth its own question.

**Hypothesis and candidate fix, neither implemented.** The AudioTrack
starts ~1.7 s before the audio stream flows: `playbackLoop` gives up on the
prefill after `STARTUP_PREFILL_TIMEOUT_MS` = 100 ms while the first real
packet arrives at ~1,800 ms (the host spawns the encoder first and
`_session_io.start(...)` after), so it writes concealment into an empty
queue and `AudioTrack.underrunCount` counts every buffer it missed. The
candidate is to hold the prefill until the first real packet with a bounded
fallback. The record names the evidence that would accept it.
