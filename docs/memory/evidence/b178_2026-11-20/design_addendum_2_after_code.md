# B178 — second addendum after code: how each side's place is chosen (found on T90 and the made-up titles)

The hashed design (section 1, step 4) chose each side's place in turn: the least spread, then the tallest. Three
changes, each because a run read the wrong place.

1. **The tallest first.** On T90 the large number under P1's emblem (36 px at 640 × 480) and the FINAL column
   (16 px) both hold in every save. The tallest has the most pixels and gives the surest read, so it ranks first,
   then the least spread.
2. **The sides chosen together, with a line height every side shares first.** On T90, P2's large number never holds:
   its second digit touches the red panel's edge highlight in the ink map, so it never sits on the line. Chosen one by
   one, P1 took its large number and P2 the FINAL column, and the two sides' digits could not be pooled. **As built:**
   every combination of the sides' best four distinct places, none overlapping; a line height every side shares
   (within 2 px) first. T90 reads both sides in the FINAL column, pooled.
3. **When the lengths do not decide, the readings do.** In a made-up title where every number has two digits, digit
   counts cannot tell P1's place from P2's. The reader learnt without one save put P1 on the right, read every label
   against the wrong glyphs, and learnt no threshold. **As built:** among those combinations, the readings' agreement
   decides. That is the mean distance of each labelled glyph to its digit's mean over every save, the same digit
   looking alike. On the made-up title: 0.007 with the sides right, 0.496 swapped. Then the places' own order.
   **One entry per distinct place:** its alignment and ink radius variants count as one place, its best.

**Also found, unchanged:** B167's anchor (learn's "screen that stays") is the frame most of the run stays near. In a
made-up run whose loading screen stayed 12 s after an 8-s final screen, the anchor was the loading screen. T90's runs
move on after their loading screens, and the made-up runs now do too. A real title whose final screen is held for
less of the 20-s run than a screen after it would be labelled on that other screen. Said in the record; the anchor is
not changed here.
