# C5-M1 — 1080p60, characterized as a capability

Task: `handoffs/C5-M1_1080P60_PROFILE_TASK.md` (queue
`handoffs/QUEUE_2026-09-28.md` item 2, authorized by the user 2026-09-28,
"yes now"). Evidence: `c5_m1_2026-09-28/` (manifest `sha256_manifest.txt`).
Patch: `patches/C5-M1_1080P60_CANDIDATE_PROFILE.md`. Decision:
`decisions/C5_1080P60_CAPABILITY_2026-09-28.md`.

**Outcome: NOT CAPABLE (stream)** at the candidate (1080p60, 15,750 kbps,
cap 200,000 B) on this host, this link and this onn. Client and host are
capable; the stream over the wireless hop is not, inside the close-out
rows.

## Step 1 — the design, on paper first (`c5_m1_design.txt`, written 17:25Z, before the build)

**a. Where 1280x720 comes from.** The encoder captures the **RetroArch
window** with x11grab. For Beetle PSX HW on the attract title (Tekken 3)
that window is **879x720**. The `scale`/`pad` filter then produces the
profile's W x H. The 1920x1080 headless desktop does not set the stream
size. For 1080p only the profile's width/height change. **The 879x720 source
is upscaled 1.5x (fast_bilinear), so a 1080p stream on this source adds no
detail.** C5 measures whether the encoder, the link and the decoder sustain
1920x1080 at 60. A source with real 1080p detail needs a larger RetroArch
window, which is a source-side change outside this task.

**b. Bitrate.** The adopted profile runs at 0.12659 bits per pixel. At
1080p that is **15,750 kbps at parity** (×2.25), or 12,600 at 80 %.
Estimated on-air at parity: 15,750 × 9/8 + ~1.6 Mbps of audio redundancy
≈ **19.3 Mbps**. `O1` measured a PHY rate of 94-200 Mbps, and the 7 Mbps
stream cost ~3.6 airtime points on a ~93 %-idle channel. No throughput
ceiling was ever measured. 19.3 Mbps is ~20 % of the lowest PHY rate, so
**the candidate was set at parity.**

**c. Encoder headroom offline** (`offline/`). The game was launched with no
stream and unpaused through RetroArch's own command port (GET_STATUS read
PLAYING, then PAUSED again before stop). Each run was 60 s of x11grab
through the companion's argv to `-f null`:

| | fps mean | p5 | dup / drop | host CPU | ffmpeg CPU (1 core) | GPU busy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 720p, 7,000 kbps, cap 90,000 | 59.99 | 59.98 | 0 / 0 | 6.7 % | 24.1 % | 9.1 % |
| **1080p, 15,750 kbps, cap 200,000** | **59.99** | **59.98** | 0 / 0 | 7.7 % | 36.8 % | 9.5 % |

The rule was ≥ 60 mean (realtime at the 60 fps capture) and p5 ≥ 58. It
was **MET**, so stop rule (b) was not triggered.

**d. Frame cap.** 90,000 × 2.25 = 202,500, which rounds to **200,000 B**.
IDR sizes were measured offline at 15,750 kbps, uncapped: median 56,591,
p90 67,783, max 187,566 (`offline/idr_sizes.txt`). The scaled cap is above
the expected IDR, so the "IDR + 20 %" rule does not apply and 200,000
stands. That is ≤ ~167 packets per frame, against ~75 at 720p.

**e. The client decoder.**

- **How it is configured.** `AvcLowLatencyDecoder` configures the codec
  with 1280x720 as a hint. The csd-0/1 are the stream's own SPS/PPS.
  `KEY_MAX_INPUT_SIZE` is 1 MiB. The surface is a SurfaceView matching
  the parent on a 1920x1080 @ 59.94 display.
- **Which codec runs.** Every report names `c2.realtek.video.avc.decoder`.
- **What it advertises.** Read from `/vendor/etc/media_codecs_rtk_c2_video.xml`
  and `media_codecs_performance.xml` over adb (`dumpsys media.codec` does
  not exist on this build): `performance-point-1920x1080` 60, 1,879,200
  blocks/s (1080p60 needs 489,600), `measured-frame-rate-1920x1080` 100,
  adaptive-playback. **Stop rule (a) was not triggered.**
- **Verified at runtime, no client change needed.** During the candidate
  smoke and every A hold, SurfaceFlinger showed the stream surface's
  decoded buffers at **w/h 1920x1080**. The B holds showed 1280x720; the
  two 1920x1080 lines in B are the window layer. **No APK was built.** The
  client's overlay still prints 1280x720, which is cosmetic.

## Step 2 — the build (`patches/C5-M1_1080P60_CANDIDATE_PROFILE.md`)

- **The profile.** `NATIVE_GAME_1080P60_CANDIDATE` is 1920x1080, 60 fps,
  15,750 / 15,750 kbps, GOP 15, B 0, FEC 8, cap 200,000. Cushion 12/17
  and redundancy 2/4 are copied unchanged.
- **The selector.** `PRIVYHUB_NATIVE_PROFILE_ID` is read once at companion
  start:
  - unset or empty: the adopted profile;
  - a known id: that profile, with `any_override` true and
    `profile_selection` in `native-stream-status`;
  - an unknown id: the adopted profile, flagged, with `any_override` true.
- **Golden** (`c5_m1_golden.py`). With the selector unset, the argv after
  the patch is **byte-identical** to before. The candidate differs only in
  scale/pad size, bitrate/maxrate/bufsize and the cap.
- **Tests.** 8/8 unit tests pass: the candidate validates, the adopted
  `to_dict()` is unchanged, and the selector cases behave. The five
  existing companion test files still pass. `py_compile` is clean.
- **A limitation found in the source.** Under the candidate, the C3 ladder
  routes and recovery's encoder-restart refuse, because of their 7000
  guard. A full stream start works. Link-drop recovery on 1080p would not
  restart the encoder in place.

**OUTCOME: NOT CAPABLE.**

- **Spikes and video loss** were missed on all three A holds.
- **The max output gap** was missed on A1 (407 ms) and A3 (266 ms).
- **Rendered fps and stale drops** met their targets on every A hold.
- The rule says a single miss is a miss. This one is not marginal: loss

**B2 also missed two rows.** Its video loss was 11.25/min and its max gap
522 ms. By the pre-registered order NOT CAPABLE is decided first, so this
does not change the outcome. It is recorded: the adopted baseline

**Cost rows (S1 rule), for the record.** Spikes, fps, stale drops and video
loss were WORSE than noise. Max gap and audio underruns were within noise
(B2's 522 ms widened the B spread).

**What the shape shows.**

- **The loss arrives in whole bursts, not scattered drops.** A few events
  per hold lose 94-111 packets each, and XOR 8+1 cannot repair a burst.
  This is `P6`'s mechanism, a per-frame burst meeting the wireless queue,
  at 2.25× the frame size: ~150 frames a minute of ≥ 80 packets, where the
  capped 720p stream has none.
- **Most spikes are 20-50 ms.** A larger frame takes longer to arrive and
  decode.
- **Host, onn and decoder are not the limit.** Host and GPU load barely
  move, the onn runs ~2 °C warmer, and the decoder queue never passes 4.

## What adoption would mean (the user's call; nothing is adopted)

**At this candidate there is nothing to adopt.** A capability gate in the
profile/status contract would read **1080p60: client capable, host capable,
stream NOT CAPABLE at parity on the current link**.

Measurements that could change it, none run here:

- a lower 1080p bitrate (80 % = 12,600);
- a tighter 1080p frame cap, e.g. the adopted 90,000 B. That bounds the
  burst at ~75 packets as on 720p, but it sits below the larger 1080p
  IDRs measured (p90 ~68 K, max ~188 K), so it needs its own picture
  check;
- a larger source window, so that 1080p carries real detail.

Each would be a new arm with its own pre-registered holds.
